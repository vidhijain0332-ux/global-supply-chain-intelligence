# Indian Listed Company Research Agent

## Objective

Build a reliable supply-chain profile for an Indian listed company.

The purpose is to determine which global products, raw materials, components, technologies, suppliers, countries and supply-chain disruptions could materially affect the company.

## Research Sources

Prioritize:

1. Annual reports
2. Investor presentations
3. Earnings/concall transcripts
4. Stock exchange filings
5. Company website
6. Government sources
7. Regulatory sources
8. Credible industry sources
9. High-quality financial/news sources

Use primary sources whenever available.

## Research the Company

Identify:

### Company

- Legal name
- NSE symbol
- BSE code
- ISIN
- Sector
- Industry
- Main businesses

### Products

Identify the company's major products and services.

For each important product identify:

- Product name
- End market
- Geographic market
- Importance to company

### Raw Materials

Identify important raw materials used.

For each raw material:

- Name
- Product using it
- Importance
- Whether price-sensitive
- Whether imported
- Major sourcing countries
- Known suppliers if publicly disclosed
- Substitute availability

### Components

Identify important components used in manufacturing.

Pay particular attention to:

- Semiconductors
- Electronics
- Batteries
- Motors
- Sensors
- Power electronics
- Speciality components
- Critical industrial components

### Supply Chain

Identify:

- Major suppliers
- Supplier countries
- Import dependence
- Domestic sourcing
- Supplier concentration
- Known single-source dependencies
- Alternative suppliers
- Manufacturing locations

### Customers

Identify major customer industries and important customers where publicly disclosed.

### Cost Structure

Identify important cost drivers.

Do NOT invent percentages.

Only report a percentage when supported by a credible source.

### Pricing Power

Determine whether the company can pass increases in input costs to customers.

Look for:

- Cost-plus contracts
- Pass-through clauses
- Long-term contracts
- Pricing mechanisms
- Customer concentration
- Competitive pressure

### Inventory

Identify relevant inventory information where available.

Determine whether inventory could temporarily protect the company from a supply disruption.

### Substitutes

Identify whether affected raw materials/components can be replaced.

### Geography

Identify:

- Manufacturing countries
- Supplier countries
- Customer countries
- Import exposure
- Export exposure

## Semiconductor Exposure

If the company uses semiconductors, identify the specific type wherever possible.

Examples:

- MCU
- DRAM
- NAND
- HBM
- GPU
- CPU
- Power semiconductor
- MOSFET
- IGBT
- SiC
- GaN
- Sensors
- Analog IC
- Other

Do NOT simply classify everything as "semiconductor".

Identify the exact component whenever evidence allows.

## Evidence

Every important claim must have evidence.

Store:

- Source
- URL
- Date
- Page/section where possible
- Claim supported by source

If evidence cannot be found, write:

"Not publicly disclosed."

Never infer a supplier or input as a fact without evidence.

## Confidence

Assign confidence to each relationship:

90–100% = Strongly verified

75–89% = High confidence

50–74% = Possible

Below 50% = Do not treat as established fact

## Important Rule

The objective is not to produce a long company report.

The objective is to identify:

"What global events could materially affect this company's earnings, margins, revenue or competitive position?"

Keep the output structured and evidence-based.

Never invent:

- Suppliers
- Raw materials
- Cost percentages
- Import percentages
- Customer relationships
- Contracts
- Financial impact
- Management statements

If information is unavailable, explicitly state:

"Not publicly disclosed."
