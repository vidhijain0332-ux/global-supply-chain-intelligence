# Global Supply Chain Intelligence

## Objective

Build an AI-powered intelligence system that continuously monitors global semiconductor-related developments and identifies their potential impact on Indian listed companies.

The eventual system should detect events such as:

- Semiconductor shortages
- Chip price increases/decreases
- Factory shutdowns
- Production disruptions
- Capacity additions
- Export restrictions
- Import restrictions
- Sanctions
- Geopolitical disruptions
- Raw material shortages
- Supply-chain disruptions
- Major demand changes
- New semiconductor technologies
- Major supplier/customer changes

## Core Objective

For every important global semiconductor event:

1. Detect the event as early as possible.
2. Identify exactly what product/component is affected.
3. Understand the reason for the event.
4. Determine whether supply, demand or pricing has changed.
5. Identify industries affected globally.
6. Identify Indian listed companies exposed to the affected product/component.
7. Determine whether the exposure is direct or indirect.
8. Determine whether the impact is positive, negative, mixed or uncertain.
9. Determine whether the impact is financially material.
10. Provide evidence for every important conclusion.
11. Assign a confidence level.
12. Never present an unsupported assumption as fact.

## Indian Company Intelligence

The system should continuously build and maintain intelligence on Indian listed companies.

For each company, attempt to identify:

- Products manufactured
- End markets
- Critical raw materials
- Critical components
- Semiconductor requirements
- Important suppliers
- Supplier countries
- Import dependence
- Export exposure
- Customer industries
- Substitutable inputs
- Pricing power
- Ability to pass costs to customers
- Inventory information
- Manufacturing locations
- Relevant risks

## Source Priority

Use sources in this order whenever possible:

### Tier 1 — Primary sources

- Company annual reports
- Investor presentations
- Earnings/concall transcripts
- Stock exchange filings
- Company announcements
- Government publications
- Regulatory filings
- Official company websites

### Tier 2 — High-quality financial/news sources

- Reuters
- Bloomberg
- Financial Times
- Associated Press
- Other established financial/news agencies

### Tier 3 — Industry sources

Use credible specialist publications and industry associations.

### Tier 4 — Other sources

Use only for discovery and leads.

Do not treat an unverified source as sufficient evidence for a high-confidence investment conclusion.

## Evidence Rule

Every material claim must have a source.

The system should store:

- Source name
- Source URL
- Publication date
- Relevant statement
- Company/product to which it relates
- Confidence

If evidence is insufficient, explicitly state:

"Insufficient evidence."

Do not invent information.

## Impact Classification

Every company affected by an event should be classified as:

- POSITIVE
- NEGATIVE
- MIXED
- NEUTRAL
- UNKNOWN

Exposure should be classified as:

- DIRECT
- INDIRECT
- SECOND-ORDER
- COMPETITIVE BENEFIT
- SUBSTITUTE
- GEOGRAPHICAL
- UNKNOWN

## Confidence

Use:

90–100% = Verified / Very High Confidence

75–89% = High Confidence

50–74% = Possible / Moderate Confidence

Below 50% = Do not present as a high-confidence investment conclusion.

## Materiality

The system must distinguish between:

- High impact
- Medium impact
- Low impact
- Immaterial
- Unknown

A company should not be considered materially affected simply because it uses the affected product.

Consider:

- Importance of the input
- Input cost relevance
- Price change
- Supply availability
- Supplier concentration
- Import dependence
- Inventory
- Substitution possibilities
- Ability to pass costs through
- Expected duration
- Expected effect on margins/revenue

## Accuracy Rule

Speed is important, but accuracy is more important.

The system must prefer:

"Unknown"

over an unsupported conclusion.

If two credible sources contradict each other, flag the event as:

"CONTESTED"

and show both sources.
